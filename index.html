About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering at Princeton University, advised by Prof. Clarence W. Rowley. I received my B.S. in Theoretical and Applied Mechanics from Peking University in 2022.

My research interests include fluid dynamics, model reduction, data-driven machine learning, uncertainty quantification, and flow control. Recently, my work centers on building data-driven reduced-order models (ROMs) of advection-dominated black-box systems for real-time control and uncertainty quantification. In particular, we are currently developing symmetry-reduced ROMs for traveling coherent structures in shift-equivariant systems such as the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation and three-dimensional channel flow.

We are also working on detecting Lagrangian coherent structures in the advection-dominated atmospheric surface layer (ASL) from sparse tracer trajectories. A longer-term goal is to use our detecting framework to understand how coherent structures organize turbulent transport in the ASL and to connect this structure-based description with prediction and flow control.

Research interests: advection-dominated phenomena · reduced-order modeling · symmetry reduction · scientific machine learning · coherent structures

Research

Symmetry-reduced model reduction for shift-equivariant systems

We develop data-driven reduced-order models of partial differential equations with shift equivariance. Such systems typically admit traveling solutions, which require many basis functions in a fixed reference frame. We instead represent the solution in a traveling reference frame, thereby obtaining a more compact representation. Existing methods such as operator inference then fit the reduced model directly from data, without knowledge of the full-order dynamics.

We are now developing a joint optimization that learns the projection subspaces and the symmetry-reduced dynamics together.

Detecting Lagrangian coherent structures from sparse data

We are investigating flow reconstruction and Lagrangian coherent-structure analysis from sparse tracer measurements in the atmospheric surface layer, with attention to the limitations imposed by finite observations.

Structured input–output analysis of wall-bounded shear flows

We studied the amplification and characteristic scales of oblique laminar–turbulent patterns in plane Couette–Poiseuille flow using structured input–output analysis.
